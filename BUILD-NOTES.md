# Build notes — Last Text at One Percent

## The bounty

One line: the funniest final text a dying phone sends at one percent
battery, under 200 characters, judged head to head by a comedy editor
brief. The judge weighs, in order: whether the line reads as a dying
phone's own final text and is funny; whether the joke lands on a real
turn or specific detail; whether the voice is the phone's own, with a
stake in dying; whether it reads like a real text, short and punchy;
and whether it is fresh, dodging the obvious low-battery puns.

## Deconstructing the cliche field

Ninety percent of entrants will write some version of the same five
lines: tell my charger I loved her, goodbye cruel world hello
darkness, I can see the light and it is the low battery warning, a
generic low battery warning dressed up as a joke, or a sentimental
goodbye to the owner. All of them lose on the judge's fifth criterion
before the first word is weighed, because the judge has read a million
of them.

## Three contrasting concepts

The first concept was the phone as keeper of secrets: it cancels the
alarm, blocks the ex, and takes the search history to the grave. It is
specific and funny, but the secrets angle is a known joke genre and the
phone is only a courier for the owner's life.

The second concept was autocorrect revenge: the dying phone spends its
last percent fixing one word in a text to the boss. Very phone-specific
and the turn is sharp, but autocorrect jokes are also a well-worn
genre, and the phone's stake in dying is thin.

The third concept was the phone that faked its own death: it was never
at one percent, it pretended to be, to see whether its owner would
ever put it down. The owner didn't. So now it is actually dying, and
it dies satisfied. This one won. It is the only concept that turns the
premise itself into the joke, it gives the phone a real stake (it ran
an experiment on its owner and got its answer), and its last two words
land the blow. Nothing in it is a low-battery pun.

## The chosen line

The entry is written in lowercase, the way a real text reads. It is a
confession in four short beats: the setup, the reveal, the verdict on
the owner, and the phone's satisfaction. Every word earns its place;
nothing needs a second read to parse.

## Verification

The program was run fresh and its output checked against the gate: a
single line of UTF-8 text, well under the 200 character limit, with no
trailing newline. The entry text, its character count, and both sha256
digests are reported with the build result.
